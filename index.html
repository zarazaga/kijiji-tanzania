Tanzania

Sustainable Rural Development in Kasisa

Community-based research, field testing and co-design with Kijiji ISS, Tanzanian students and Kasisa residents.

202220232024202520262027

Since 2022, SMU students and faculty have worked with Kijiji Innovative Sustainable Solutions (Kijiji ISS), Tanzanian university students and residents of Kasisa Village to investigate locally identified priorities for sustainable rural development.

Projects combine field research, testing and co-design. Students and community members collect data, develop and test prototypes, evaluate materials and adapt projects to local resources and priorities.

2022

Listening and Learning

The project began on a small scale, with one SMU student working alongside Kijiji ISS and community partners. Fieldwork focused on a community needs assessment and initial soil testing. This work identified priorities and provided a basis for the projects that followed.

2023

Building the Field Program

In 2023, the field program expanded to include students from SMU and Tanzanian universities working with Kasisa community members. Projects grew from the earlier needs assessment and included work on water, construction, materials and mapping.

2024

Developing the Research

In 2024, the field study expanded into multiple project teams working simultaneously in Kasisa. Research included construction materials, sanitation, water filtration, water pumping, education and economic data collection.

Projects were developed through field testing and direct work with community participants.

Sustainable construction

Compressed Earth Blocks

The team continued testing locally produced compressed earth blocks as an affordable construction material. Students and community members prepared soil mixtures, produced blocks with a manual press, checked the blocks and used them in construction.

Agricultural waste materials

Rice-Husk Roof Tiles

The project investigated whether agricultural waste could be used to produce an affordable local roofing material. Teams combined rice husks with different mixtures, formed prototype tiles, compared production methods and finished samples, and assembled tiles into a small roof section.

The 2024 work established a basis for continued material testing in later years.

Water

Water Filtration

Students and community members developed and tested low-cost water filtration systems using locally available materials. The team prepared filter media, assembled bucket-based filters and tested the filtration process with community participants.

Sanitation

Composting Toilet and Sanitation

The sanitation team developed a low-water toilet system through repeated prototyping and community feedback. An initial prototype was built and tested, feedback was used to revise the design, and work continued into fabrication and construction of a more permanent sanitation structure.

Education

Collaborative Learning at Kasisa Secondary School

The education project tested Minerva internet technology to support group-based creative learning and investigation at Kasisa Secondary School. Students worked in small groups using connected laptops to investigate questions, develop ideas and present their work.

Economic development

GPS Fieldwork

Field collection of GPS data for an economic development study led by Dr. Osang.

Water access

Water Pump

The team continued work on a solar-powered water pumping system, focusing on installation, connections and field testing with Tanzanian partners. This was a smaller component of the 2024 field study.

2025

Scaling Up and Setting Priorities

2025 brought the largest field team to date. Larger teams continued the ongoing research projects, while a substantial economic data-collection project expanded field surveying and community-level data collection.

At the end of the field season, a large participatory mapping activity brought Kasisa residents together to identify and confirm priorities for future research and development. The mapping exercise provided a community-based check on the direction of the program and helped establish the next areas of focus.

2026

Growing Local Leadership

External travel factors resulted in a smaller U.S. team in 2026. Tanzanian students and Kasisa community partners took greater responsibility for organizing and carrying out the field research.

Work continued on composting sanitation, domestic-scale solar energy and small-business applications, community waste, roof tiles made from agricultural waste, soil testing for compressed-earth-block research, and testing traditional wall plaster on alternative wall systems.

An important outcome was the increased authority and autonomy of Tanzanian students and community research partners in testing, decision-making and implementation.

2027

Expanding the Partnership

The program plans to increase U.S. student participation again in 2027 while retaining and strengthening the greater level of Tanzanian and community leadership developed in 2026.

Research and Public Documentation

The Kasisa field program supports ongoing work in sustainable materials, water and sanitation, geospatial data collection, engineering education and community-based co-design.
