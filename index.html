Kijiji Sustainable Rural Development

Research in Kasisa, Tanzania

Long-term field research, testing and community co-design with Kijiji ISS, Tanzanian students and Kasisa residents.

Since 2022, SMU students and faculty have worked with Kijiji Innovative Sustainable Solutions, Tanzanian university students and residents of Kasisa Village on locally identified priorities for sustainable rural development.

The work is organized here by project so that each research thread can be followed across multiple field seasons.

Site & Water

Understanding the site and essential systems

Site Mapping

Precision mapping and field data collection for land use, planning and project development.

2022

The first mapping work tested a high-accuracy RTK device at the Kasisa site and established methods for later precision field surveys.

2024

RTK/GPS field measurement expanded as part of site and cadastral data collection.

Water

Water access, storage and pumping systems developed and tested in the field.

2022

Early work addressed water procurement and storage, including construction of a water tank at the site.

2024

The team continued field installation and testing of a solar-powered, low-energy water pumping system with Tanzanian partners.

Water Filtration

Low-cost filtration using locally available materials.

2024

Students and community members prepared filter media, assembled bucket-based filters and tested the filtration process. Community participation helped assess both operation and suitability for local use.

Toilets & Sanitation

Low-water sanitation developed through prototyping, testing and community feedback.

2024

The team built and tested an initial composting toilet prototype, gathered community feedback, revised components and continued into construction of a more permanent sanitation structure.

2026

Development of the composting toilet system continued with a smaller U.S. team and greater responsibility taken by Tanzanian students and community partners.

Materials & Construction

Testing local materials and building systems

Compressed Earth Blocks

Locally sourced soil tested and developed for durable, lower-impact construction.

2022

Initial soil testing began to assess locally available material for compressed earth block construction.

2023

Soil testing and community workshops continued. A CEB press was brought from Dar es Salaam and the team produced the first successful blocks.

2024

Students and community members prepared soil mixtures, produced blocks with a manual press, checked dimensions and construction quality, and used more than 500 blocks in construction.

2025–2026

Research continued with refinement of block recipes, building tests and soil-testing methods for the CEB climate-impact study.

Sustainable Roof Tiles

Roofing materials developed from agricultural waste and other locally available materials.

2024

Teams combined rice husks with different mixtures, formed prototype tiles, compared production methods and finished samples, and assembled tiles into a small prototype roof.

2026

Material development continued using agricultural waste, including testing local clay and plantain waste as possible raw materials for new roof tiles.

Sustainable Construction

Field testing of building assemblies, local materials and alternative construction methods.

2024

The construction team worked alongside the CEB project on locally appropriate building systems and hands-on construction at the Kasisa site.

2026

The team tested a traditional wall plaster made from waste on alternative wall systems.

Landscape Design & Drainage

Site planning, landscape design and drainage responses to local conditions.

2025

Field teams investigated drainage issues and site conditions as part of the continuing work on climate impact and site development.

Climate & Energy

Measuring conditions and testing energy systems

Climate Data

Weather and environmental data collected to understand local conditions and their effects on buildings, materials and drainage.

2025

Weather-data collection and drainage investigation were developed as part of the field study.

2026

Climate-focused work continued, including refinement of soil testing for the CEB climate-impact study.

Solar Energy

Small-scale solar systems linked to household needs and local economic activity.

2024

Solar power supported the low-energy water-pump project.

2026

The team developed a domestic-scale solar system together with small-business applications.

Community, Education & Economics

Community priorities, learning and economic data

Community Co-Design Process

Community priorities and local knowledge guide the direction, testing and evaluation of the research.

2022

The program began with a community needs assessment to identify priorities before the larger field program was developed.

2024

Community members worked directly with project teams in prototype testing, construction, material evaluation and field decisions.

2025

The largest field team to date concluded its work with a large participatory mapping exercise. Kasisa residents mapped concerns and priorities and helped confirm the areas for future research and development.

2026

External travel factors resulted in a smaller U.S. team. Tanzanian students and community partners took greater responsibility for organizing research, testing, decisions and implementation. The program plans to increase U.S. participation again in 2027 while retaining this greater level of Tanzanian and community leadership.

Education and Technology

Collaborative learning and investigation using digital tools in local schools.

2024

The team tested Minerva internet technology at Kasisa Secondary School. Students worked in small groups using connected laptops to investigate questions, develop ideas and present their work.

Economic Development

Community-level data collection supporting research on local economic conditions and opportunities.

2024

Field collection of GPS data for an economic development study led by Dr. Osang.

2025

A substantially larger team expanded the economic data-collection project and community-level field surveying.

Continuing field research

2025 and 2026 photographs, measurements and project results will be added to these project histories as the archive is developed.
